## What Is the Vossi Bop Dance?

The move is deliberately lazy: lean slightly forward, roll your hands in front of your chest, clap, and step side to side on the beat, shoulders doing most of the work. That looseness is the whole appeal — it looks effortless, which is exactly why it spread.

## What Does Vossi Bop Mean?

"Vossi" is NL Vossi, the dancer the move is named after, and a "bop" is a dance. So to Vossi bop is to do his move. In Stormzy's song it is a boast: "My bruddas don't dab, we just Vossi Bop" — his crew skipped the dab, the dance everyone was doing at the time, for this newer, homegrown one.

## Who Created the Vossi Bop Dance?

The dance is credited to NL Vossi (Twitter handle @NL_Vossi, since suspended), whose short clips of the move went viral in 2015. The choreography spread as user-generated videos because it needed almost no skill to join in.

## How Is Stormzy Connected to Vossi Bop?

Stormzy came across the dance online and loved it — he told Beats 1 it was "so infectious, and such a genuine joyous moment". He recorded a track named after the move and released "Vossi Bop" on 25 April 2019 as the lead single for his album *Heavy Is the Head*. The music video opens with him alone on an empty Westminster Bridge at dawn, Big Ben behind him, doing the bop while the camera circles — that exact shot is what the 2026 AI trend copies. The song became his first UK number one as a lead artist.

## How Do You Do the Vossi Bop Dance?

Four beats, four parts:

1. Lean slightly forward and loosen the shoulders.
2. Roll both hands in front of the chest.
3. Clap once.
4. Step to each side on the beat, letting the shoulders lead.

## Why Did Vossi Bop Become Popular?

Three reasons stack up. The move is easy — almost anyone can join in after one watch. The song was enormous: "Vossi Bop" debuted at UK number one with the year's biggest first-week streaming numbers at the time. And the dance translated perfectly to short video, moving from Twitter clips in 2015 to YouTube recreations in 2019 to TikTok routines, where bedroom versions and the "puffer jacket" clip kept it alive long after the song left the charts.
