## What the clip actually does with your photo

Knowing the shot helps you pick the photo. The template runs one continuous rising take, about ten seconds:

1. You stand on the promenade in your own clothes, looking up.
2. Two giant golden hands reach down and take your hands.
3. A flash of white light bursts at your chest — your outfit becomes long, flowing white garments.
4. The hands lift you while the camera climbs. The promenade, the beach and the hotel towers fall away below. You look down, then up, mouth open, then laughing.

Your photo only controls one of those four beats: the person. The hands, the flash, the lift and the coastline are all generated from the template, which is why one photo is the entire production budget.

## The photo that survives the render

I have rendered enough of these to know where the failures come from, and almost all of them are the input photo:

- **Leave headroom above the head.** The golden hands reach in from the top of the frame. A photo cropped tight to the face gives them nowhere to enter, and cramped entries are the number-one cause of distorted hands. Waist-up or full body, with open sky above the hair.
- **Keep the arms in frame.** The hands hold your hands. If your shoulders and arms are cropped out, the model has to invent where they are, and invented limbs look invented.
- **One adult per photo, face clear.** Daylight, no sunglasses, no heavy beauty filter. A sharp front-facing face is what stays recognizable after the white clothes go on.
- **Background does not matter.** Your kitchen, your street, a parking lot — all fine. The promenade and the sea are generated.

One thing not to do: photos of minors, celebrities, or anyone who did not agree to be in the video. Those get rejected by moderation, and they are the kind of posts that get accounts in trouble.

## The mistakes that actually break renders

Four failures cover most bad outcomes:

- **Tight close-ups.** Covered above — no headroom, no arms, awkward hands. Fix the photo, not the settings.
- **Blurry or filtered faces.** The model copies what it can see clearly. A soft, dark or heavily filtered face drifts after the outfit swap, and the person in the white clothes stops looking like you.
- **Group photos.** The scene holds exactly one person. Two faces in the upload means the model merges or picks one, and both results look wrong.
- **Expecting the heaven ending.** The original TikTok post continues past the lift into a heaven scene and a second lift above a cruise ship. The template recreates the promenade lift — the ten seconds everyone quotes. If your render ends above the coast, that is the whole format, not a bug.
