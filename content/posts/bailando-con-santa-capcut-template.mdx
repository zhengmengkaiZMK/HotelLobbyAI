**Bailando con Santa** — Spanish for "dancing with Santa" — is the template that started the Dance with Santa trend. If you saw a video of someone dancing next to a very committed Santa Claus on a red carpet and wondered where it comes from, this is the answer, plus the way to make the same video without installing anything.

To make one right now, open [the Dance with Santa template](/dance-with-santa-ai-template). For the trend history, read [what is the Dance with Santa trend](/blog/what-is-dance-with-santa-trend).

## What "Bailando con Santa" means

The name is Spanish: *bailando con Santa* = dancing with Santa. The searches it answers include *baila con santa claus*, *bailando con santa claus*, and *bailando con santa claus plantilla* (plantilla = template) — they all point to the same format: one photo, fifteen seconds, you and Santa dancing side by side.

The template was posted by **@sarahicat15**. Per ViralRemix's timeline, the first version went up on 7 November 2026, and the second version — with its own sound and the one that spread — on 9 November. The sound is "original sound - sarahicat15", a cheerful Latin Christmas tune. Most of the earliest posts came from Guatemala and Mexico, captioned only #CapCut, which is why the first wave of Dance with Santa videos is in Spanish.

## How big it got

By ViralRemix's 15 November 2026 check, the template had passed **44,000 uses — about 7,200 new ones a day** — and the sound about 4,100 videos, roughly 670 new a day. The biggest organic clips: a little girl in a woven skirt at about 67K plays, the burgundy satin dress at about 57K, a pink-tee mom at about 28K. Tutorials grew even faster: a Spanish how-to reached about 164K plays and an English one about 268K. Numbers move daily; treat them as a snapshot.

## The scene, which never changes

Every clip runs the same fifteen seconds: red carpet, wood-panelled hall, two tall decorated trees, wrapped presents, fairy lights, blue arched windows. Santa — white beard, red velvet suit, black belt and boots — waves at the camera, then dances the routine with the person from the photo: bounce, hand behind the head, knee lift, hip shake, turn, smiles. The very first template clip is 14 seconds; every version after runs 15.

## CapCut template or browser — what differs

The CapCut template runs inside the CapCut app: paste one photo into the placeholder and it renders on your phone, carrying the template's sound. ViralReel runs the same trend in the browser at [the Dance with Santa template](/dance-with-santa-ai-template): one full-body photo, one of eight casts sourced from the trend, and a 15-second 9:16 MP4 — no app to install, paid exports without a watermark, failed jobs returning their credits, and no licensed soundtrack in the file (add the trend sound in TikTok, Reels, or Shorts either way).

Whichever path you take, label the post as AI-generated and use photos of people who agreed to be in the video.

## FAQ

**Is Bailando con Santa the same as Dance with Santa?** Yes — one is the Spanish template name, the other is the English trend name. Same video format.

**Do I need to speak Spanish to join?** No. The first wave was Spanish, but the format is visual; English tutorials and posts caught up within a week.

**Why do kids' clips dominate?** A kid copying Santa's moves is the version grandparents share most — the woven-skirt girl is the biggest clip in the trend. On ViralReel, a child's photo may only be uploaded by that child's parent; the rules are on [the kids version page](/dance-with-santa-kids).

**Is the Santa real footage?** No. Santa is an AI-rendered character in the template; only the person next to him comes from a photo.
