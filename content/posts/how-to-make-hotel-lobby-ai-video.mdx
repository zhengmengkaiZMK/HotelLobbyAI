## What is the Hotel Lobby AI template?

The Hotel Lobby AI template recreates the famous orange-booth performance stage entirely with AI. You upload two front-facing photos — one per performer — and the model stages both subjects on set, syncs their lips and gestures to the music, and renders a vertical 9:16 video that is ready to post.

Every element people recognize from the viral videos is baked in: the orange paneling, the single hanging microphone, the intimate two-shot framing. The format works because it is instantly recognizable (viewers stop scrolling when they see the booth) and because it is a two-person format — which means both performers share the result, doubling your audience.

## Step 1: Pick the right photos

Photo quality decides video quality. The single biggest reason Hotel Lobby AI videos look off is a weak source photo. Follow these rules:

- **Front-facing only.** The model needs to see both eyes and the full face shape. Profile shots, extreme angles, and heavily tilted headshots produce distorted results.
- **Even, soft lighting.** Avoid harsh shadows across the face and avoid backlit photos where the face is dark.
- **One subject per photo.** Group photos where faces overlap confuse identity matching. Crop to a single person.
- **Keep sides consistent.** If performer A is on the left in your first photo, keep them on the left in every photo of that person across sessions. This helps the AI keep identities stable between renders.
- **Plain background helps.** You do not need a studio, but a clutter-free background reduces artifacts.

Photos of pets, babies, and original characters work too — see our notes on [variant formats](/blog/viral-ai-duo-video-formats) below.

## Step 3: Set resolution, ratio, and duration

In the generator dialog you can tune three parameters before generating:

- **Aspect ratio** — 9:16 for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. 16:9 for YouTube. 1:1 for feed posts.
- **Resolution** — 480p, 720p, or 1080p. 720p is the sweet spot for social; choose 1080p for the sharpest export (supported models only).
- **Duration** — 8, 12, or 15 seconds. Longer is not better for this format: the trend favors tight loops.

If something looks wrong, the fastest fix is usually a better source photo, not a prompt rewrite.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Using sunglasses, masks, or heavy motion blur in source photos.
- Uploading a photo where the subject is tiny in the frame.
- Picking 16:9 for TikTok — vertical 9:16 is the native format of this trend.
- Deleting the source photos too early: renders sometimes need a second attempt with the same inputs.
- Giving up after one video. The format rewards iteration.
