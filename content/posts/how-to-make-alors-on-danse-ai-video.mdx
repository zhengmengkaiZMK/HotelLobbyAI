The **Alors On Danse AI** trend drops two new faces into the 2010 white-studio sketch — one person deadpan at the mini keyboard, one dancing and pointing at the camera — set to Stromae's 2009 hit. This guide walks through making your own version in a few minutes with the [Alors On Danse AI template](/alors-on-danse-ai-template): two photos in, a full 30-second 16:9 video out. If you want the backstory first, start with [what Alors On Danse AI is](/blog/what-is-alors-on-danse-ai).

## What you need before you start

1. **Two photos** — one for each role. The keyboard player should be a clear front-facing shot (that face gets the most screen time at the keys); the dance partner can be front-facing or full-body. JPG, PNG and WebP all work.
2. **The right to use both faces.** Use your own photos or those of an adult who agreed to it. Do not upload minors, celebrities, or strangers grabbed from the original clip.
3. **That is it.** No app, no editor, no prompt writing — the scene, the moves and the beat are built into the template.

## Step 1 — Open the template and upload two photos

Open the [Alors On Danse AI template](/alors-on-danse-ai-template) and upload your two photos. The slots are labeled:

- **Keyboard Player** — the deadpan one at the mini keyboard. A calm photo works best; the AI needs a face it can keep straight.
- **Dance Partner** — the one who bursts in and takes over the room.

Photos with even lighting and no sunglasses or heavy filters track the animation best.

## Step 2 — Pick one of the seven takes

The trend has a whole family of versions, and the template ships all of them:

1. **Studio Giant** — the signature white-studio take, closest to the original sketch.
2. **Original Sketch** — the compact two-hander staging.
3. **Football Duo** — the DJ Yamal / MC Messi style football take.
4. **Spider Duo** — the superhero pair.
5. **Fantasy Duo** — the Gandalf-and-Frodo corner of the trend.
6. **Racing Duo** — the F1 version that started the first reswaps.
7. **Football Keyboard** — the footballer-at-the-keyboard variant.

Each take keeps the same law of the sketch: one dances, one does not care.

## Step 3 — Generate the 30-second clip

Keep the aspect ratio at **16:9** — unlike the vertical trends, this meme was born on TV, and the white studio frames wide. The video runs the **full 30 seconds** of the sketch's rhythm, so there is room for the entrance, the dance and the deadpan payoff. Hit generate and wait for the render; download the MP4 as soon as it finishes.

## Step 4 — Add the song, label it AI, post natively

The MP4 carries the picture only. To finish the post:

1. In TikTok, Reels or Shorts, add **"Alors on danse"** (the original or the Extended Mix) from the in-app sound library — the synth hook is what files your clip under the meme.
2. Turn on the platform's **AI-generated label** when asked. The sketch belongs to Jamel Debbouze and Stromae; fan parodies are fine with the label on and no claim that the original actors are in the clip.
3. Post natively — cross-posting tools add a watermark and throttle reach.

## Tips that separate good versions from great ones

- **Cast against type.** The funniest swaps put the calmest face at the keyboard and the most chaotic one on the dance floor — or the reverse.
- **Render two or three takes** and post in batches; a run of posts beats one "perfect" clip.
- **Lead with the contrast.** If your caption can set up "one tries, one doesn't", the clip lands harder.
- **Watch the credits.** If you repost a version you did not make, credit the creator who made it.

## FAQ

**Do both photos need to be the same person?**
No — the sketch needs two different people. Two different faces is the default; a single person playing both roles works as a gag if the template take allows it.

**Why 16:9 and 30 seconds when every other trend is vertical and short?**
Because this meme recreates a TV sketch, not a phone video. The white studio was staged wide, and the bit needs its full runtime.

**Can I use the song inside the MP4?**
No — the render is silent of the track. Add the original song from the TikTok sound picker so your post is properly credited and filed under the meme.
