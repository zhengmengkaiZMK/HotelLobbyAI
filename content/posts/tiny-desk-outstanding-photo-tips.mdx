## The three slots

The generator does not ask you to mark a saxophone. It asks for three people:

1. **Center Lead** is the singer in the middle. Use the face you want viewers to recognize first. A front-facing photo, eyes visible, head and shoulders in frame, works better than a full-body shot where the face is a speck.
2. **Left Role** is one side of the band.
3. **Right Role** is the other side.

The sax solo lives in that band scene. The render does not promise that Left Role, Right Role, or Center Lead is the person holding the saxophone. Upload three different people when you want three different faces. Repeating one photo three times makes a weaker band.

Reference models require all three photos. JPG, PNG, and WebP are the formats the how-to already names. The how-to is [how to make a Tiny Desk Outstanding AI video](https://www.viralreel.app/blog/how-to-make-tiny-desk-outstanding-ai-video).

## Lighting

Even light on the face matters more than a clever background. The template replaces the room.

- Face the camera. A slight angle is fine. A profile or the back of a head gives the model almost nothing to lock onto.
- Put the light in front of the person. A window behind them turns the face into a silhouette.
- Keep both eyes visible. Heavy sunglasses, a hand over the mouth, or hair that covers the face make the lead harder to read. The Uncle Charlie *set* includes shades as a costume on the template. That is not an instruction to upload a photo where the eyes are hidden.
- Skip beauty filters, face-swap previews, and screenshots of a screen. A photo of a photo adds a second frame the model has to fight.

## What to leave out

Do not upload:

- Minors.
- Celebrities, including Charlie Wilson, NPR hosts, coaches, players, and owners. The All-Loss, Cowboys, NFC East, Rams, and Spurs sets are costumes for your own people. They do not generate those public figures.
- Screenshots or crops of the NPR Tiny Desk concert, the original TikTok, or a news article about it.
- Group photos. The slot wants one face. A crowd makes the model guess.
- Photos you do not have permission to use. A tagged picture from someone else’s camera roll is not permission.
