## Which photo is which

The generator labels the slots Keyboard Player and Dance Partner. Order is the joke.

1. **Image 1, Keyboard Player.** This face stays at the keys and does not break. A clear, front-facing photo is enough. Chest-up works. Even light matters more than a clever background, because the template replaces the room with the white studio.
2. **Image 2, Dance Partner.** This person dances, points at the lens, and commits far too hard. A photo that is already smiling, or that shows more of the body, keeps more of them. Full body is useful here. It is not required for Image 1.

Swapping the two files does not break the render. It flips the joke: the calm face dances and the chaotic face sits still. Check the buttons before you generate. ## What the camera should see

- Face the camera. A slight angle is fine. A profile, or the back of a head, gives the drawing almost nothing to lock onto.
- Put the light in front of the person or the pet. A window behind them turns the subject into a silhouette.
- Keep the eyes visible on the keyboard photo. Heavy sunglasses, a hand over the mouth, or hair that covers the face make that straight expression harder to read.
- Skip beauty filters and screenshots of a screen. A photo of a photo adds a second frame the model has to fight.
- JPG, PNG, and WebP are the formats the how-to already names. The minimum the generator states is 240×240.
