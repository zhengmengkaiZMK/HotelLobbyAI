You have probably seen it already: a steel elevator, strip lights on the ceiling, one person waiting inside, and a second person who walks in with their back to the camera, turns round, puts in an earbud, and then mouths a love song straight at the lens like nothing is happening. That is the 365 Elevator trend, and it runs on one song — "365" by the German singer Sampagne.

The good news: you do not need to edit anything. The whole clip is a fixed scene, which means two photos is the entire production budget. Here is the exact workflow I use, including the part most people get wrong — which photo goes in which slot.

## What you need before you start

- Two photos of two different people (or you twice, if you want the "then vs. now" version — more on that below)
- A free afternoon slot of about ten minutes

That is the whole list. No app to install, no CapCut project, no prompt writing. The scene never changes between versions — that is the point of the format — so the only thing you actually contribute is the casting.

## Which photo goes in which slot

This is the one thing worth getting right before you spend credits, because the slots are order-sensitive.

**Slot 1: the person already inside the elevator.** They stand on the right of the frame, they are there when the doors open, and they start the lip-sync first. On the 365 Elevator page, the upload button reads "Elevator singer".

**Slot 2: the person who walks in.** They enter through the sliding doors with their back to the camera, step to the left, turn round, put in an earbud, brush their hair back and glance at their phone. The button reads "Walk-in guest".

If you swap them by accident, the video still renders — your singer just ends up walking in, which kills the joke. Swap the photos and render again; failed or wrong renders cost you one retry, not the evening.

## Step by step

**1. Pick your duo.** You and your partner. You and your best friend. Your boss and your intern (with their permission, obviously). **2. Take the right kind of photos.** The clip shows both people from head to knee, standing. A tight selfie gives the model almost nothing to work with. What works: front-facing portraits, framed from the chest or knees up, even light, eyes clearly visible, one person per photo. Wear the outfit you want to appear in — the render copies it.

## The photo mistakes that actually break renders

I have watched enough of these renders to know the four failures that come up again and again:

- **Swapped sides.** Covered above — slot 1 waits inside, slot 2 walks in.
- **The face smears during the turn.** The walk-in involves a 180-degree pivot. Side-angle photos give the model weak facial landmarks after the turn, so the face drifts. Straight-on photos fix it.
- **Sunglasses.** The overhead strip lights glare off the lenses and the eyes stop being readable. Skip them.
- **Leftover details from the original duo.** A cap, a printed vest, a scarf that belongs to nobody in your photos. This means the reference identity was not fully replaced — one more render with sharper portraits usually clears it.

## A note on the song

"365" is by Sampagne (@sampagnebaby), and the artist's name is spelled Sampagne, not Champagne — a mistake the comments correct daily. The hook is "365 Tage": 365 days, as in the singer would wait every day of the year for someone. That waiting-room romanticism is why an elevator works as a stage, and why the trend's sound page is where all the view counts pile up. One search trap worth knowing: the Netflix film "365 Days" also has a famous elevator scene, and it shows up in the same searches. That is a different thing entirely — a film clip, no walk-in, no duo of your own.
