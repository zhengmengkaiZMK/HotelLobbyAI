**Dance with Santa** is the Christmas trend where one photo becomes a 15-second video of that person dancing next to Santa Claus — on a red carpet, in a wood-panelled hall full of trees and presents, doing every one of Santa's moves. This page is the trend explainer: where it came from, how fast it grew, and what the numbers said when we checked.

If you want the maker instead of the history, go straight to [the Dance with Santa template](/dance-with-santa-ai-template). If you want the full walkthrough, read [how to make a Dance with Santa AI video](/blog/how-to-make-dance-with-santa-ai-video).

## What happens in the video

Every clip in the trend shares one locked scene. A cozy wood-panelled Christmas hall: red carpet, two tall decorated Christmas trees, wrapped presents, warm fairy lights, blue arched windows. A classic Santa Claus — full white beard, red velvet suit with white fur trim, black belt and boots — stands beside the person from the photo. Santa waves at the camera, then the two dance side by side in sync: a bounce, a hand behind the head, a knee lift, a hip shake, a full turn with their backs to the camera, and big smiles to finish.

The scene never changes. The person next to Santa is the only variable — which is why the format reads instantly and why every family member has become a cast: moms in their best dress, daughters posting their little sisters, and someone always doing their grandma.

## Where it came from

The trend comes from a CapCut template called **"Bailando con Santa"** — Spanish for "dancing with Santa". Per ViralRemix's timeline:

- **7 November 2026** — @sarahicat15 posts the first CapCut template, "Bailando con santa", with a clip of a woman in a red varsity jacket dancing next to Santa.
- **9 November 2026** — she posts a second version with its own sound. This is the one that spreads.
- **11 November 2026** — the first results go up as plain #CapCut posts, mostly from Guatemala and Mexico; a mom in a pink tee reaches about 28K plays.
- **13 November 2026** — the little girl in a woven skirt dancing next to Santa reaches about 67K plays, and a Spanish tutorial by @rora_dd about 164K.
- **14 November 2026** — English tutorials follow; one reaches about 268K plays.
- **15 November 2026** — the template passes 44,000 uses, about 7,200 new ones a day, and its sound about 4,100 videos.

All figures are per ViralRemix's 15 November 2026 check; trend numbers move daily.

## Why it went viral

Three forces stack up. **First, the input is minimal** — one photo, which is the lowest-effort entry of any Christmas trend this season, while the output looks like a family memory you never filmed. **Second, the format is locked** — same hall, same Santa, same fifteen seconds — so every remake is readable in one second and the only variable is the dancer. **Third, the cast is universal**: toddlers, grandmas, formal-wear moms. It is a family WhatsApp share before it is a TikTok trend, and it arrived exactly when Christmas-card season starts and people want something better than a static portrait.

## What the sound is

The spreading template version carries "original sound - sarahicat15", a cheerful Latin Christmas tune. Renders recreate the moves, not the track — add the sound from the TikTok, Reels, or Shorts library when you post. The song question, and why there is no download, is on [the Dance with Santa song page](/dance-with-santa-song).

## What it is not

- **Not the Santa Muerte dance.** That is a different trend — quinceañera-style videos with La Santa Muerte, the skeleton folk saint, often set to Santa Grifa's "Bailando con la Muerte".
- **Not the "Here Comes Santa Claus" dance.** That one is filmed by real people in pajamas; it has no photo template.
- **Not a mall Santa video.** The Santa in these clips is an AI-rendered character built into the template.
