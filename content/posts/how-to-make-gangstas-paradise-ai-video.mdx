## What you need before you start

1. **Two photos of two different people.** Photo 1 is the rapper — a sharp, front-facing shot, because that face gets the most screen time at the microphone. Photo 2 is the singer — a photo where the whole head is visible; a waist-up or full-body shot helps, because that performer walks through the scene. JPG, PNG and WebP all work, and each photo needs at least 300×300 pixels.
2. **The right to use both faces.** Upload your own photos or those of an adult who agreed to it. Do not upload minors, celebrities, or strangers grabbed from someone else's clip.
3. **A few minutes.** A render usually finishes in about 2–3 minutes. The exact credit cost is shown before you generate, and failed or canceled jobs are refunded automatically.

The two upload slots are labeled **Rapper photo** and **Singer photo**. The casting matters more here than in most templates:

- **Rapper photo (photo 1)** carries the verse — close-ups at the mic, walking shots, a point at the camera. Use the sharper, more front-facing photo of the two.
- **Singer photo (photo 2)** walks past silently, then steps in for the chorus. A full-body or waist-up photo tracks the walk better than a tight crop.

Pick two people who look clearly different — during fast cuts it keeps the roles easy to follow, both for the model and for your audience.

## Step 3 — Check the settings before you generate

The template is intentionally low-touch. Three things to check:

- **Duration: 20 or 30 seconds.** The reference performances are staged with a verse, a walk-past and a chorus payoff, so both lengths give the bit its full runway.
- **Aspect ratio: keep it on auto.** The reference clips are 4:3 landscape, and auto lets the output follow their framing. If you specifically want a vertical post, you can pick 9:16, but the staged wide scenes read best in their native frame.
- **Resolution: 720p or 1080p**, depending on the engine the template offers that day.

You do not write a prompt. If a prompt box is visible, the template text is already filled in — the scene, the mic work and the choir cue are built in.

## Step 4 — Generate, then check the performance

Press **Generate** and keep the window open — a job usually takes about 2–3 minutes. When the MP4 is ready, check it before posting:

1. **Both faces still match their photos** after every cut — longer clips with several shot changes give the model more chances to drift.
2. **The mouths follow the verse** — the performance motion is tracked from the reference clip, so the rapper should be rapping and the singer should land the chorus.
3. **The choir payoff lands** — that final rise is the beat the format lives on.

If a render drifts, generate it again; a failed or canceled job returns its credits, and a second run with the same two photos often lands cleaner.

## Photo tips that separate good versions from great ones

- **Cast against type.** The funniest swaps put the most ordinary-looking person on the mic and let the staging do the drama — or the reverse.
- **Even lighting beats retouching.** A flat, well-lit face tracks better than a heavily filtered one; sunglasses, heavy shadows and extreme angles all cost accuracy.
- **Render two scenes and post in batches.** A run of posts beats one perfect clip — the five takes exist so you can test which stage lands with your audience.
- **Keep the photos different in tone.** Two near-identical selfies blur together once the cuts start.

## FAQ

**How long does a Gangsta's Paradise AI video take to make?**
About five minutes end to end: a minute to pick a scene and upload two photos, and roughly 2–3 minutes for the render. Longer 30-second takes can take a little more.

**Which scene should I pick first?**
Galactic Hangar — it is the staging the meme started from, and the choir payoff is the most readable. If you want something less sci-fi, Silver Rapper and Tuxedo Duet are the most "real world" of the five.

**Why did the singer's face drift in the second half?**
Long clips with many cuts ask the model to rebuild the face at every change. Use a sharper source photo, or trim your plan to the 20-second take, which has fewer shot changes to hold.

**Do I need to write a prompt?**
No. The template carries the scene, the motion and the structure. The prompt field, when shown, is pre-filled — you only touch it if you want to add casting notes.

**Can both photos be the same person?**
It works as a gag, but the format is built for a duet: the verse performer and the chorus performer appear in the same frame at the payoff. Two different people read much better.
