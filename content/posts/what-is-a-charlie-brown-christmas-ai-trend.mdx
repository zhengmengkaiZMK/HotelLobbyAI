**A Charlie Brown Christmas** is the TikTok trend of the 2026 Christmas season: you upload one photo, a bright scan line sweeps from your head to your feet, and behind the sweep the photo becomes a **1965 Peanuts-style cartoon** — big round head, two small black dot eyes, thick even ink outlines, flat colors with no shading — standing full-body on **Snoopy's red doghouse** in the snow, with a hand-lettered *"A Charlie Brown Christmas"* title across the top.

The trick that makes it work: the cartoon is still obviously **you**. Same hairstyle, same glasses (the dot eyes sit under your own frames), same beard, same jacket and its print. It is less a costume than a translation — and it became the family-friendly AI trend of the season, the rare one grandparents and kids post for the same reason.

## What an A Charlie Brown Christmas video looks like

Every clip runs the same three-beat structure:

1. **The before shot.** Your real photo fills the frame — waist-up or full body, face to the camera — with the white hand-lettered *"A Charlie Brown Christmas"* title across the top.
2. **The scan line.** A bright horizontal line sweeps from the top of the head down to the feet. Above the line, the photo is already flat 2D cartoon; below it, still the real you.
3. **The cartoon.** Big round head, dot eyes, short-line eyebrows, a small curved smile, short arms and legs — but your exact hairstyle, glasses, and outfit colors and prints.
4. **The landing.** The finished character stands on Snoopy's red wooden doghouse — red sloped roof, dark plank lines, a green wreath with a red bow, snow on the edges — facing the camera.
5. **The caption.** Most posts say nothing but the number of days left until Christmas. That countdown format is the whole gag.
6. **The honesty badge.** Posts carry the AI-generated label, and the biggest accounts keep it on.

## Who started the trend, and when

The trend runs on a **CapCut template named "A Charlie Brown"**. The first post was a mirror selfie by **@jason_l_smith** on **22–23 October 2026**, captioned *"I know Christmas is still some time away"* — it stayed under 600 plays. The first post that took off was **@nanceesjourney** on **26 October 2026**, captioned *"Sixty days till Christmas!"* — the moment the Christmas countdown became postable, it pulled near 70,000 plays within days.

Three days later the trend exploded. On **29 October 2026** a tutorial by **@sm5bhbjno8** passed 270,000 views and **@lisahoesman**'s selfie passed 139,000, with most of the posts on the trend's sound arriving that same day. By 1 November the CapCut template had logged roughly **23,700 uses** and was still gaining about **1,700 a day**, while the **#charliebrownchristmas** hashtag sat near **253 million views**.

## The 1965 special it borrows from

The look comes from ***A Charlie Brown Christmas***, the Peanuts TV special that first aired on **CBS on 9 December 1965** — directed by Bill Melendez, scored by the **Vince Guaraldi Trio** (*"Christmas Time Is Here"*, *"Linus and Lucy"*), and famous for its flat hand-drawn cel style at a time when nothing on television looked like it. The trend's red doghouse with the wreath comes from the scene where Snoopy decorates his doghouse for the Christmas play. Even the title card is borrowed: the filter's lettering is the special's own hand-lettered opening.

The characters and the special belong to **Peanuts Worldwide and the Charles M. Schulz estate** — nothing in the trend is official, from the CapCut template to every TikTok to [the ViralReel template](/charlie-brown-christmas-ai-template).

## Why it went viral

Three forces stack:

1. **The input costs nothing.** One photo. The template holds the scene, the move, and the sound.
2. **The output is a Christmas post about you.** Which friend looks like which cartoon. Your grandfather as a Peanuts character. The family in the same order they stood. It is not a filter you hide behind — it is a post that says something.
3. **Recognizability is the joke.** The redraw keeps your glasses, hair, and outfit, so the funniest posts are the ones where the dot eyes sit under very familiar frames.

And unlike most AI trends, nobody looks bad in it. The 1965 style flatters everyone — which is why casts widened within days: couples, whole families, grandparents, Navajo elders, a Star Trek Kirk-and-Spock pair, and pets (full-body shots only — a headshot of a black cat comes back as a grey blob).

## What it is *not*

- **Not the Charlie Brown dance trend.** That was December 2025: people recreating the special's awkward dance scene to a GloRilla x Vince Guaraldi mashup.
- **Not the Spanish-language "foto con Snoopy" wave** from October — those photos went photorealistic inside Snoopy scenes without being redrawn.
- **Not an official Peanuts filter.** There is no such thing. It is fan work end to end.
